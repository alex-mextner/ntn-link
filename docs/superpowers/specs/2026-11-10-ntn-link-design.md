# ntn.link: pretty links for public Notion pages

Status: implemented and deployed (production https://ntn-link.vercel.app, main `2480b5d`, 2026-11-10). This document describes what is built; section 10 lists what is verified and what is not. The brand and domain `ntn.link` appear in examples; until a domain is bought the service runs on `ntn-link.vercel.app` (section 3). Date: 2026-11-10.

## 1. Goal

Turn a public Notion URL into a short readable link with a clean preview card, with no registration:

`https://app.notion.com/p/thsm/413d447ac4b180169985c93eea7f02f9?source=copy_link` -> `https://ntn.link/thsm/our-trips`

Every link has two forms:

- **Redirect link** `https://ntn.link/<ws>/<slug>`: clean preview card, then the real Notion page.
- **Fast link** `https://ntn.link/<ws>/<slug>/read`: an instantly loading, static, zero-JavaScript copy of the page, built to the existing fast-page standards (section 5.3).

Success criteria:

1. A message containing the pretty link shows a clean card in Telegram, Slack, WhatsApp, Discord, X and iMessage: page title without the "| Notion" suffix, page description, a 1200x630 image (the page's own social image, or a generated title card).
2. A human opening the redirect link lands on the real Notion page after one HTML hop (a 200 document that redirects immediately; section 5.2).
3. Three interfaces, all without an account: web page, CLI, browser extension.
4. Nobody can take over another workspace's namespace by guessing it or by reading a public challenge.
5. Hosted on Vercel, running cost close to the domain price.
6. The fast link loads instantly everywhere. Measurable acceptance on the owner's test page (`thsm`, "Наши с Леночкой Путешествия") on a Vercel preview: Lighthouse mobile performance >= 95; LCP, INP and CLS all in Google's "good" range (LCP <= 2.5 s, INP <= 200 ms, CLS <= 0.1); TTFB and total transfer clearly below the Notion baseline measured on 2026-11-10: TTFB about 0.29 s (median of 5 runs 0.28-0.70 s), HTML 22.7 KB, 16 first-party JS/CSS files referenced by that HTML totalling 1,322,907 bytes on the wire (brotli; 6,076,505 bytes decoded), plus the 15 KB (brotli; 50.7 KB raw) page-data call. Targets: fast link HTML <= 14 KB compressed (fits the first TCP congestion window), no JavaScript, total transfer excluding images <= 30 KB (about 2% of the baseline), TTFB from the edge cache below the 0.29 s baseline when measured the same way from the same machine.

Non-goals: private pages, analytics, click counts, custom domains for the user, password protection, an official Notion OAuth integration, Google AMP and Yandex Turbo pages (section 5.3 gives the reasons).

## 2. Evidence this design rests on (probed 2026-11-10 against the owner's `thsm` page)

- Notion serves complete OpenGraph tags to a bot user agent: `og:title` ("Наши с Леночкой Путешествия | Notion"), `og:description`, `og:image` (`...?width=2000`, a custom `SocialMediaPreviewImage.png`), `og:site_name` ("thsm on Notion"), `og:locale` = `en_US`. The problem is Notion branding and the 2000 px image, not missing data. A plain `curl` without a bot user agent gets the generic Notion `og:image` (`app.notion.com/images/meta/default.png`), so metadata fetches must send a bot user agent.
- Every URL form redirects to the canonical `https://<workspace>.notion.site/<32-hex-id>`: `app.notion.com/p/thsm/<id>`, `www.notion.so/thsm/<id>`, `www.notion.so/<id>`, and slug-prefixed `.../Our-Trips-<id>`. The workspace domain is therefore recoverable from the final URL after redirects.
- A page that is not public (or does not exist) answers 404 with no OG tags. This is the "is it public" test.
- `notion.site/<id>` without a workspace subdomain redirects to the Notion homepage. Workspaces without a domain are not supported (see risks).
- The page body is not in Notion's server-rendered HTML; the Notion client loads it from an unofficial API. `POST https://thsm.notion.site/api/v3/loadPageChunk` with the request shape used by `notion-client` (`{"page":{"id":"<uuid>"},"limit":100,"cursor":{"stack":[]},"chunkNumber":0,"verticalColumns":false}`) answered HTTP 200 without cookies or any token, three times (0.30-0.38 s each). Payload: 50.7 KB raw, 15.1 KB brotli, 21 block records on the test page: `page` 9 (root plus sub-pages), `header` 4, `text` 3, `divider` 1, `collection_view_page` 1, `collection_view` 1, plus two records without a type; the root page lists 18 child blocks. `loadCachedPageChunkV2` also answered 200 (51.7 KB). The test page has no `image` blocks, so image blocks inside content are unverified; only the cover and the social preview image were probed. Database contents (`collection_view`) need a further `queryCollection` call that was not probed.
- Images: the cover is a relative Notion asset (`/images/page-cover/webb2.jpg`, 200, 151,664 bytes under `thsm.notion.site`). The social preview is stored as `attachment:<uuid>:SocialMediaPreviewImage.png`. It resolves two ways: `GET https://thsm.notion.site/image/<urlencoded attachment>?table=block&id=<blockId>&width=1200` answers 302 to `img.notionusercontent.com/s3/...?tok=<JWT>` (the JWT's `exp` is about 24 hours ahead), and `POST /api/v3/getSignedFileUrls` returns a `file.notion.so/f/f/...&expirationTimestamp=...&signature=...` URL (expiring, about 6 hours ahead in the probe). Either way image URLs are signed and expire, so they must never be copied into stored data or served pages as is.
- `notion-client` and `react-notion-x` (NotionX, repo `NotionX/react-notion-x`) are both licensed MIT (`npm view` and the repo `license` file; copyright Travis Fischer); both at version 8.0.8 on 2026-11-10. They are unofficial and unaffiliated with Notion.

## 3. URL model

`https://ntn.link/<workspace>/<slug>` (redirect) and `https://ntn.link/<workspace>/<slug>/read` (fast).

- `<workspace>` is the Notion workspace domain, read from the canonical URL. It is never typed by the user.
- `<slug>`: `[a-z0-9-]`, 1-64 chars, unique inside the workspace. Default is a transliteration of the page title ("Наши с Леночкой Путешествия" -> `nashi-s-lenochkoy-puteshestviya`); the user can edit it. Reserved words (`api`, `_next`, `og`, `img`, `new`, `about`, `read`, ...) are rejected as slugs and as workspaces.
- Domain status: **launch on `ntn-link.vercel.app`** (the default origin of the CLI and the extension, and the production `PUBLIC_ORIGIN`). `ntn.link` ($7.72 per year registration and renewal per Porkbun's public price list; no RDAP record and no DNS delegation found on 2026-11-10) is the intended permanent domain and `ntn.page` ($10.81) the fallback; neither is bought, because availability and final price are confirmed at registrar checkout, which needs the owner (money). The switch touches three constants: `PUBLIC_ORIGIN` (Vercel env), the CLI `DEFAULT_ORIGIN` (`packages/cli/src/config.ts`) and the extension build origin (`NTN_LINK_ORIGIN`); it is tracked in UB-145.

## 4. Ownership without accounts

Namespaces are claimed by proving control of the Notion workspace, not by being first. The challenge has two halves so that publishing it on a public page does not hand it to anyone who reads that page.

1. The client calls `POST /api/challenge {url, action}` with `action` = `claim` (namespace unclaimed) or `rotate` (key lost or replacing it). The server returns two values: a public `nonce` (`ntn-verify-<10 chars>`) and a private `claimToken` (random, 256 bit). The `claimToken` is never written to any page; it stays in the requesting client's memory or storage. The server stores only its SHA-256 hash.
2. The user temporarily puts the `nonce` into the title of any public page of that workspace (the page being linked works) and presses Verify.
3. `POST /api/verify {url, nonce, claimToken}`. The server requires both: it checks that the challenge exists (`challenge:<ws>:<nonce>`), is not expired, is bound to this exact namespace and to the requested action, and that the hash of `claimToken` matches (constant-time compare). Then it fetches the page under that exact `<ws>.notion.site` as a bot and looks for the `nonce` in `og:title`. Finally it deletes the challenge atomically; only the request whose delete succeeds proceeds, so a concurrent duplicate cannot succeed twice.
4. On success: `claim` sets the namespace atomically (`SET NX` on Redis, `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` on Postgres), `rotate` replaces the key hash with a compare-and-set; a random 256-bit owner key (`ntnk_` prefix) is returned once. Only its SHA-256 is stored (`ns:<ws>` -> `{keyHash, claimedAt, rotatedAt}`). The CLI keeps the key in `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/ntn-link/keys.json` (mode 0600, per service origin and workspace), the web page and the extension keep it in browser storage.
5. Later create/update/delete calls in that namespace send the key as `Authorization: Bearer`; no re-verification.
6. A challenge is single use with a 15 minute TTL (`CHALLENGE_TTL_SECONDS`). Someone who reads the public `nonce` still cannot claim without the `claimToken`. A wrong `claimToken` does not consume the challenge (otherwise a reader of the nonce could burn it) but counts toward a cap of 5 attempts (`CHALLENGE_MAX_ATTEMPTS`), after which the challenge is deleted. A nonce that is not (yet) on the page does not consume the challenge and costs no attempt, because Notion may still be publishing the edit; `verify` is rate limited per IP instead (20 per 10 minutes).
7. Recovery of a lost key is a brand new challenge with `action=rotate`; no earlier nonce, token or key is ever reused or accepted. A rotate challenge is bound to the key version it was issued against (`namespaceKeyHash`): once another rotation replaced the key, older challenges fail with `challenge_expired`. If storage cannot confirm whether a rotation was applied, the request fails closed (no possibly dead key is returned, the challenge is consumed, and a new rotate challenge always recovers).
8. **Sweep after rotation.** Every existing link in the namespace is re-fetched and deleted if its target no longer resolves publicly under this workspace (covers a workspace domain that changed hands); a transient Notion failure keeps the link. A sweep call is bounded (`DEFAULT_SWEEP_BUDGET`: 20 s, at most 100 fetches, 8 in flight) so it fits a serverless function. A link confirmed public is stamped with the key version it was swept for and is skipped by later calls; a link whose fetch failed is retried behind links with fewer failures. The rotation response reports whether the sweep was complete; if not, the owner finishes it with the idempotent `POST /api/ns/<ws>/sweep` (rate limit 10 per 10 minutes; CLI `ntn-link sweep <ws>`).

Why: anyone who sees `thsm.notion.site/...` can otherwise create `ntn.link/thsm/anything` first. The title challenge proves edit access to a published page of that workspace, which is the same authority that can publish pages. The private `claimToken` ties the proof to the party that started the flow.

All links, and therefore all fast pages, exist only inside a namespace claimed this way.

## 5. Components

Monorepo, Bun workspaces, TypeScript strict.

- `apps/web` (Next.js App Router on Vercel): web UI, JSON API, the redirect route, the fast-page route (edge cached, section 5.3), the image proxy route, the generated card image route.
- `packages/core`: pure logic shared by all three clients and the server: Notion URL parser, slugifier with Cyrillic transliteration, OG HTML renderer, metadata normalizer, ownership state machine, and the Notion block renderer (blocks to static HTML, section 5.3).
- `packages/cli`: `ntn-link`, one file, no runtime dependencies, run as `npx ntn-link <url>`.
- `extension/`: Manifest V3 for Chrome and Firefox.
- Storage (`Store` interface in `packages/core/src/store.ts`; selection in `apps/web/lib/store.ts`): **PostgreSQL on Neon** when `DATABASE_URL` (pooled URL) is set, through the Neon HTTP driver (stateless, one HTTPS request per statement, fits serverless). Production runs Neon `ntn-link-db` in Frankfurt, attached through the Vercel Marketplace integration; its variables exist for **Production only**, not Preview or Development, so previews and local runs can never touch the production database. Fallback: **Upstash Redis** (`UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_*`, or the `KV_REST_API_*` names the Marketplace injects; resolved per variable, `UPSTASH_*` wins) when no `DATABASE_URL`. With neither configured, development falls back to an in-memory store with a warning and **production fails loud**: every storage-touching route answers a typed HTTP 500 `server_misconfigured` rather than keeping data in a function's memory. Postgres layout: table `ntn_kv(key text primary key, value text, expires_at timestamptz)` and `ntn_set_members(set_key, member)`, created lazily and idempotently (`CREATE ... IF NOT EXISTS` under an advisory lock; an already provisioned database costs one probe query). Expiry uses the database clock. Keys: `ns:<ws>` -> `{keyHash, claimedAt, rotatedAt}`; `link:<ws>/<slug>` -> `{workspace, slug, pageId, canonicalUrl, title, description, imageUrl, language, fetchedAt, createdAt, overrides, fast, canonicalMode, goneAt, sweptVersion, sweepTried}`; `nslinks:<ws>` (set of slugs, cap 200 links per namespace, enforced under a per-set advisory lock); `pagelock:<ws>/<pageId>` (creation lease, makes create idempotent per page); `challenge:<ws>:<nonce>` -> `{workspace, action, claimTokenHash, namespaceKeyHash, expiresAt}` plus an attempts counter, 15 minute TTL, deleted on success; `rl:<action>:<hash>` rate limit counters. Stored values are validated on read: a corrupt record is a fault (`server_misconfigured`), never "missing" (a corrupt `ns:` record must not look unclaimed).
- **Storage timeout and warm-up policy.** Neon's free plan scales to zero after about 5 minutes idle. Every storage request has a 3 s deadline (`DEFAULT_TIMEOUT_MS`); a failure becomes a typed HTTP 502 `upstream_error` and one redacted log line (class, reason and command name only, never keys, values, SQL or the connection string; at most one identical line per 10 s). To survive the cold start, after an idle gap longer than 240 s (and on first use) the store runs an idempotent `SELECT 1` with a longer 10 s deadline before the real statement; concurrent callers share one warm-up and a failed warm-up is not remembered. Routes that touch storage declare `maxDuration = 60` so the warm-up plus Notion calls (10 s deadlines) fit.
- **Storage contract on Neon.** The shared store contract suite also runs against the real database (`bun run test:neon`, opt-in) with the production deadline. One database call is one HTTPS connection to a small compute, so 220 calls at once do not fit the 3 s deadline even for plain reads; the per-namespace cap case therefore bursts 20 calls at a time and asserts the same cap invariants (never more than 200 links). Consequence for clients: one owner firing a hundred creations at once can get typed `upstream_error` for the tail; the CLI and the extension create links one at a time (UB-149 tracks writing down and checking this limit).

### 5.1 API

| Call | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `POST /api/preview {url}` | Parse and fetch; returns normalized card data, the proposed slug and whether verification is needed. Stores nothing. |
| `POST /api/links {url, slug?, ownerKey?, fast?, overrides?}` | Create. Without a valid key in an unclaimed or foreign namespace returns `needs_verification`; the client then calls `/api/challenge`. Returns both the redirect link and the fast link. |
| `POST /api/challenge {url, action}` | `action` is `claim` or `rotate`. Returns `{nonce, claimToken, expiresAt}`; the `nonce` is public, the `claimToken` is private to the caller. |
| `POST /api/verify {url, nonce, claimToken}` | Checks and consumes the challenge; on success claims or rotates and returns the owner key. |
| `PATCH` / `DELETE /api/links/<ws>/<slug>` | Update slug, text overrides, the `fast` switch, `canonicalMode`, or delete; owner key required (DELETE also accepts the operator `X-Admin-Token`, section 7). Each triggers on-demand purge of the link's cached forms. |
| `GET /api/ns/<ws>/links` | List links of a namespace; owner key required. |
| `POST /api/ns/<ws>/sweep` | Finish the post-rotation sweep (section 4.8); owner key required, idempotent. |

Errors are typed (`invalid_request`, `not_notion_url`, `not_public`, `unsupported_workspace`, `slug_taken`, `slug_reserved`, `slug_invalid`, `needs_verification`, `unauthorized`, `namespace_claimed`, `namespace_not_claimed`, `challenge_expired`, `challenge_invalid`, `nonce_not_found`, `link_limit`, `rate_limited`, `not_found`, `gone`, `upstream_error`, `not_implemented`, `server_misconfigured`; the source of truth is `packages/core/src/api-types.ts`) and each carries a human message that the web UI, CLI and extension print as is. Per-IP rate limits (fixed windows, `rate-limit.ts`): preview 30/60 s, links 30/60 s, challenge 10/10 min, verify 20/10 min, sweep 10/10 min; `rate_limited` answers 429 with `Retry-After`.

### 5.2 Serving the redirect link: `GET /<ws>/<slug>`

One behaviour for every client, with no user agent detection:

- HTTP 200 with a tiny HTML document: `og:title`, `og:description`, `og:image`, `og:url`, `twitter:card=summary_large_image`, no schema.org markup. Humans are sent on by `<meta http-equiv="refresh" content="0;url=<canonical Notion URL>">` plus an inline `location.replace()`, with a visible link in `<noscript>` as fallback.
- Tradeoff, accepted: a redirect page costs one HTML hop instead of a 302, a few tens of milliseconds slower with a brief blank flash. In return, a 302 cannot carry OG metadata at all, and any user agent allowlist silently breaks for every new crawler. One behaviour for every client means no classifier to maintain and no unfurler gets a worse card than another.
- `Cache-Control: public, max-age=0, s-maxage=3600, stale-while-revalidate=86400` and a per-link `vercel-cache-tag`, so the Vercel edge serves it instantly and refreshes in the background. Updates and deletes purge every form of the link on demand (the redirect page, the card image and `/read`). A missing link answers 404 with `s-maxage=60`.
- Metadata comes from the stored record and is refreshed lazily in the background when older than 24 hours, so renamed pages follow. Overrides win over fetched values.
- Title: `og:title` with a trailing ` | Notion` removed. `og:site_name` is omitted so the platform shows our domain; `og:locale` follows the detected page language; `twitter:card=summary_large_image`.
- **Image: `/img/<ws>/<slug>`, 1200x630, with these rules.**
  - *Never Notion's generic banner.* When a page has no cover of its own, Notion puts its own marketing images in `og:image`: the white "Notion" logo or wordmark from the static `/images/meta/` folder (`default.png`, `notion-wordmark.png`, ... under many names and hosts) and the per-page "Made with Notion" card (`SocialMediaPreviewImage.png`). Both count as "no image". Detection has two layers (`packages/core/src/notion-placeholder.ts`): the URL shape before anything is downloaded, and the SHA-256 of the downloaded bytes for a known banner re-published under an unseen name; a redirect from an innocent URL to a banner is caught too. A real cover (an upload, or a picture from Notion's cover gallery) never matches.
  - *Generated card.* Without a usable image the route draws a 1200x630 PNG with `next/og` and bundled fonts with Cyrillic coverage: the page **title plus the beginning of the page text** (the first lines of the first data chunk, emoji removed because the bundled fonts have none and nothing may be fetched at render time, whole rows only so nothing is cut mid-sentence by the frame). The web preview card uses the same planner (`planCardText`), so it shows exactly the lines the card carries (`/api/preview` returns the `excerpt`).
  - *Real covers pass through.* A usable Notion image is proxied byte for byte (nothing is decoded in our process) when it is PNG or JPEG, at most 8 MiB, from an allowlisted image host, with an 8 s deadline; anything else (SVG, WebP, oversized, other host, failure) falls back to the generated card. The source URL is resolved fresh on every request that misses the CDN because Notion signs image URLs for 6-24 h: only the bytes are cached (CDN, `s-maxage=3600, stale-while-revalidate=86400`, link tag) and a short in-memory per-instance TTL cache holds the resolved URL, never the store. A stored URL is only a fallback for a transient Notion failure, and a stored "Made with Notion" card is not served even then. A link whose page is no longer public gets the generated card.
  - Known gap: pass-through images carry no exact-size guarantee (tracked in UB-144).
- If the target stops answering 200: the link is marked gone and every client gets a small "this page is no longer public" page (410) with no redirect.

### 5.3 Fast variant: `GET /<ws>/<slug>/read`

Purpose: a link that itself serves an instantly loading copy of the page, following the standards that exist today rather than a single vendor format.

**Rendering.** The server renders the Notion page into static, semantic, zero-JavaScript HTML. Data comes from `notion-client` (MIT) or its request shape (`loadPageChunk`, section 2), and rendering is our own lightweight renderer in `packages/core`. We do not use react-notion-x's renderer: its JavaScript and CSS weight defeats the purpose. Supported blocks, at least: paragraph (`text`), headings (`header`, `sub_header`, `sub_sub_header`), bulleted and numbered lists, `to_do`, `quote`, `callout`, `code`, `divider`, `image`, `toggle` (rendered as `<details>`), `bookmark`, `table`, `column_list`, and sub-pages (`page`) as links. Any other block, including `collection_view` databases, becomes a visible "open in Notion" link to that block; no block is ever silently dropped.

**Delivery.** Edge caching with explicit headers (the same mechanism as the redirect link), not Next ISR, so one purge path covers both link forms: `Cache-Control: public, max-age=0, s-maxage=60, stale-while-revalidate=86400, stale-if-error=86400` with the link's `vercel-cache-tag`; updates and deletes purge through the service `purge` callback (`revalidatePath` plus tag purge). A transient Notion failure throws (502) instead of rendering an error page, so the edge keeps serving the last good copy; a missing link or `fast` off is 404 and a page that stopped being public is 410, both with `s-maxage=60`. Known gap: a cached `/read` of a deleted link can outlive the delete at the CDN (observed live, tracked in UB-144). Per page:

- **Zero JavaScript.** The only `<script>` is the JSON-LD data block. Inline CSS (no `url()`, no `@import`), system font stack by default, no web fonts, no third-party requests. **CSS budget 10 KB** (it was 3 KB for the plain layout; now about 6.9 KB raw, 2.1 KB brotli), asserted in `packages/core/test/render/page.test.ts`. Compressed first HTML <= 14 KB where the content allows.
- **Notion-like look.** Typography, spacing and palettes are measured from Notion's own public pages: 704 px text column, page icon and cover, 40/30/24/20 px headings, callouts, toggles and toggle headings, nested bulleted/numbered/to-do lists, quotes, code, tables, columns, bookmark cards, page links with icon, table of contents, captions, Notion's nine colours, light and dark (`prefers-color-scheme`), and the per-page options full width, small text, serif/mono. Details and measurements: `docs/fast-variant.md`.
- **Known unsupported blocks** (they stay "Open in Notion" links or plain text, never silently dropped): `collection_view` databases, embeds, video, files and PDFs, synced blocks, equations, external object mentions, bookmark thumbnails, syntax highlighting in code blocks, and pages beyond 5,000 blocks or 40 chunks ("this page continues"). Known look gaps (table-of-contents labels for mention, date and equation headings) are tracked in UB-147.
- Images go through our own proxy route (`/img/content/<ws>/<slug>/<blockId>?v=<edit time>&w=<640|1280>`; covers and uploaded icons `?k=cover|icon` for the link's own root page only), which resolves a fresh signed Notion URL server-side (signed URLs expire, so they never appear in the HTML), serves only image blocks on the link's own page, refuses non-raster content, lets Notion's own resizer (`width=`) produce a modern format by `Accept` (no `sharp`), and caches `s-maxage=30 days, immutable` keyed by the block's edit version. Every `<img>` has explicit `width` and `height` (the block's recorded size, otherwise read from the image header at render time, otherwise 16:9); the first image is eagerly loaded with `fetchpriority="high"`, later ones `loading="lazy"`.
- `rel=canonical` points to the fast page itself by default; the owner can switch it (`canonicalMode`) to the Notion URL.

**Standards satisfied, and why:**

| Standard | Why |
|---|---|
| Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP, CLS) | Google's page experience standard; the only speed metric Google ranks on. Static HTML, no JS and explicit image dimensions make all three trivial. |
| Open Graph and Twitter cards | The same tags as the redirect link, so previews of the fast link are equally clean. |
| schema.org `Article` JSON-LD | Machine-readable title, description, image and dates for search engines. |
| Semantic HTML (`article`, `h1`-`h3`, `figure`, `details`, `table`) | Readability-style extractors and Telegram Instant View parse it reliably. |
| `rel=canonical`, default self | Gives the fast page its own identity; the owner can point it at Notion instead. |
| Telegram Instant View template for `ntn.link/*/*/read` | Telegram's own fast-page standard. Because the HTML is uniform, one template at instantview.telegram.org covers every page. Submission and approval are outside our control, so this is a spike with a defined fallback: without it the page is still fast and has OG. |

**Not built, and why:**

- Yandex Turbo pages: shut down in April 2025 (announced by Yandex Webmaster on 2025-02-07).
- Google AMP: since June 2021 it is not required for Top Stories and gives no ranking boost; Core Web Vitals are the standard. AMP is restrictive and costly to maintain.
- Telegraph mirroring: Telegraph pages get Instant View automatically but support only a small tag set; an Instant View template over our own HTML is cleaner and keeps one source of truth.

**Security and abuse.**

- The fast variant exists only for links created by a verified namespace owner (section 4).
- All Notion text is HTML-escaped; rich text never produces raw HTML.
- Images come only from allowlisted Notion image hosts (`notion.site`, `*.notion.site`, `file.notion.so`, `img.notionusercontent.com`, `www.notion.so/images`, and `images.unsplash.com` for Notion's stock covers). An image from any other host is not proxied and renders as a link.
- Per-link `fast` switch (default on): turning it off makes `/read` return 404 and purges the cache.
- Copyright and phishing laundering: a mirror hosts copied content on our domain, which makes laundering and takedown claims our problem. Operator delete (`ADMIN_TOKEN`, section 7) removes the link and disables the mirror at the same time.

## 6. Interfaces

- **Web (`/`)**: English only (every string lives in `apps/web/lib/client/strings.ts`). One input. Paste -> live card preview (the card an unfurler would see, in Telegram, Slack and X styles; for a page without a cover, the generated card with title and start of the page) -> editable slug -> customize title and description -> `fast` toggle (default on) -> Create. If verification is needed, a second step shows the public `nonce` with a copy button and a Verify button; the `claimToken` stays in the page's memory. The result shows both links, the short redirect link and the fast link, each with Copy. "My links" is read from browser storage plus the list call.
  - **Focus and paste capture rule** (`apps/web/lib/client/focus.ts`, pure and unit tested): the URL field is always ready. Focus is only reclaimed when it is on nothing (`body`/null), on an element nobody can act on (a script-focused heading, a disabled control), or after a state change that removed the focused control; it is never taken from another field, button, link, summary or dialog, never while a dialog is open, never from a text selection the user may be about to copy (except after a state change), and on touch devices only after a state change (so the on-screen keyboard can be dismissed). A paste with text while nothing can receive it is captured: the trimmed text goes into the URL field and the preview starts at once; a paste into another field, button or dialog is left alone; a paste into the URL field previews after the browser inserts it. A printable key pressed with focus on nothing is typed into the field; Escape clears it. A mouse click on Copy hands focus back to the field, keyboard activation and touch taps do not.
- **CLI** (`npx ntn-link`, one package, no runtime dependencies): `ntn-link <url> [--slug s] [--fast] [--copy] [--origin URL]` prints only the redirect link on stdout; with `--fast` it prints the fast link instead (the fast page is created by default). Subcommands: `ls <ws>` (tab-separated `slug`, `title`, `link`), `rm <ws>/<slug>`, `sweep <ws>`, `set <ws>/<slug> [--title] [--description] [--slug] [--fast on|off] [--canonical self|notion]`. Progress, prompts and errors go to stderr; typed exit codes (0 ok, 2 usage, 3 verification needs a terminal, 5 page not linkable, 6 slug, 7 not authorized, 8 rate limited, 9 challenge failed, 10 network, 11/12 sweep unfinished/unsupported, 1 other). Origin: `--origin`, else `$NTN_LINK_ORIGIN`, else `https://ntn-link.vercel.app`. Each run writes a 0600 log to `/tmp/ntn-link-<timestamp>-<pid>.log` that never contains keys or claim tokens. The key file is updated under a lock that survives killed runs, and a key is never sent to another origin. If the challenge nonce was put on the page being linked, the CLI asks to remove it again and checks Notion no longer shows it before creating the link.
- **Browser extension** (Manifest V3; Chrome 116+, Firefox 128+; host permissions only for Notion domains and the service origin): toolbar button and `Ctrl+Shift+L` copy the pretty link, `Ctrl+Shift+F` the fast link; the options page holds owner keys per workspace (claim with the challenge flow, or paste a key the CLI made) and the per-workspace checkbox **Replace Notion's Copy link**, off by default. The replacement was proven feasible (a MAIN-world wrapper of `Clipboard.prototype.write`/`writeText`) and is gated: only a single Notion page URL of a workspace that is switched on and has a stored key, and only after the extension itself proves the page public (a direct credential-less request to `<ws>.notion.site/api/v3/syncRecordValuesMain`) within one 3 s deadline; any failure writes Notion's original clipboard text. Nothing is sent until the user acts. See `extension/README.md`.

## 7. Security

- Only these hosts are ever fetched for page metadata and page data, over HTTPS: `notion.so`, `www.notion.so`, `notion.site`, `*.notion.site`, `notion.com`, `www.notion.com`, `app.notion.com`. Redirects are followed manually, each hop re-checked against the allowlist, max 5 hops, 5 s timeout, 1 MB body cap. This blocks SSRF. Image fetching by the proxy follows the narrower image host list in section 5.3.
- The redirect target is always the canonical `https://<ws>.notion.site/<id>` rebuilt by the server, never a user-supplied string, so the service is not an open redirector.
- Notion titles, descriptions and page text are attacker-controlled: HTML-escaped in OG output and in the fast page, rendered as text in the web UI.
- Per-IP rate limits on preview, create, challenge, verify and sweep (section 5.1); a per-namespace cap of 200 links.
- Phishing laundering through public Notion pages is a real abuse path for any short-link service, and the fast mirror adds copied content (section 5.3). Mitigation in v1: operator delete via an `ADMIN_TOKEN` header on the delete route, which also disables the mirror; no automated moderation.
- No accounts, no analytics, no IP storage beyond rate-limit counters.

## 9. Risks and open points

1. Workspaces without a workspace domain are unsupported; I have only seen domain-bearing workspaces and nonexistent ids. Needs one real example to confirm what such a page redirects to.
2. Verification depends on Notion's server-rendered `og:title`; an unofficial but stable-for-years behaviour. The live smoke test is the alarm.
3. Copy interception in the extension depends on how Notion performs the copy; it fails closed to Notion's own text.
4. A workspace domain that is renamed and later reused by someone else is handled only at key rotation (section 4.8), not continuously.
5. The domain purchase needs the owner; the service launched on `ntn-link.vercel.app` (section 3).
6. The unofficial page-load API (`loadPageChunk`, `syncRecordValuesMain`) may change or be rate limited without notice; the fast variant has no official alternative without an integration token. `packages/core/test/render/live.test.ts` (`LIVE=1`) is the alarm; on failure fast pages keep serving the last cached render and the redirect link is unaffected.
7. Block coverage gaps: databases and the other blocks listed in section 5.3 render as "open in Notion" links.
8. Telegram Instant View approval is outside our control; the template is a draft (`docs/fast-variant.md`) and the fallback is the fast page with OG tags.
9. Mirror content liability: copied page text and images served from our domain (section 5.3 security).
10. Neon free plan scales to zero: the first request after idle pays the warm-up (section 5), up to 10 s.
